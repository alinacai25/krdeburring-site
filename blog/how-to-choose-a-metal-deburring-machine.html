How to Choose a Metal Deburring Machine

Published 2026-09-09 · Selection Guide

Choosing the wrong deburring machine costs more than the purchase price — it costs throughput, surface quality and rework. This guide walks through the five decisions that matter.

1. What burrs are you actually removing?

Burr size is the single biggest driver of machine type.

Burr sizeTypical sourceRecommended machine
Small burrs + surface finishMedium/low-power laser cutting, thin sheetRoller chamfering (R series)
Small + large burrs, chamferingMixed laser/plasma partsBelt + roller (BR series)
Large burrs on thick steelHigh-power laser, plasma, flame cuttingDouble belt (BB series)

Rule of thumb: if your burrs exceed 2 mm, one belt is not enough — you need the double-belt design where the rough belt removes the big burr and the fine belt finishes the rest.

2. What material are you processing?

  • Carbon steel (thick, hard burrs) → double-belt (BB series) handles heavy burrs and scale.
  • Stainless steel, aluminum, copper → roller-based finishing gives the smooth, uniform hairline these materials demand.
  • Mixed materials in one shop → belt + roller (BR series) is the flexible middle ground.

3. What working width do you need?

Match the machine width to your largest common part, not your largest possible part.

Working widthModels
800 mm800BR / 600BB
1000 mm1000BR / 1000BB
1300 mm1300BR / 1300BB
1600 mm1600BR

4. How much automation do you want?

  • PLC control (BR series) offers repeatable, recipe-driven processing with minimal operator training.
  • Button control (BB series) is simpler and lower-cost for high-volume, single-process work.

5. What's your budget and payback?

Manual deburring is slow, inconsistent and labor-heavy. A machine that runs at 6 m/min feeding speed typically pays for itself within months through labor savings and rework reduction — not years.

Step-by-Step Selection Checklist

  1. List your three highest-volume parts and their burr size, material and max dimension.
  2. Determine whether you need deburring only, or deburring + chamfering + finishing.
  3. Match to a machine type using the tables above.
  4. Confirm working width against your largest part.
  5. Verify power (voltage), dust extraction and footprint against your factory.
  6. Request a sample test — any serious manufacturer will run your parts before you buy.

FAQ

What's the difference between deburring and chamfering?

Deburring removes sharp, unwanted edges and burrs; chamfering deliberately cuts a beveled edge. Many machines, like the BR series, do both in one pass.

Can one machine handle both thin stainless steel and thick carbon steel?

Yes — a belt + roller machine (BR series) is designed for exactly this range, from thin stainless to thick carbon steel.

How fast is machine deburring compared to hand grinding?

Feeding speeds up to 6 m/min replace slow, inconsistent manual grinding — often 5–10× faster with uniform results.
